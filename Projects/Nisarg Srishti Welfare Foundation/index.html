Nisarg Srishti Welfare Foundation

Nisarg Srishti Welfare Foundation (NSWF) is a non-governmental organization (NGO) based in Pune, Maharashtra, India, founded on 11 January 2023.[1] The organization describes its work as focused on raising awareness of noise pollution, promoting environmental conservation, and supporting women's empowerment and education in India.[1]

History[edit]

NSWF was founded on 11 January 2023 by Garima Kavathekar and Deepali Kavathekar.[1] Garima Kavathekar is described by the organization as a social worker and creative professional, and Deepali Kavathekar as a retired medical professional.[1] The organization's core team also includes Gaurav Kavathekar, an engineer credited with developing a horn-reduction device called "Control Honk," which the organization states is undergoing the patent process.[1]

Mission and Vision[edit]

According to the organization, NSWF's stated mission is to work towards human welfare, women's empowerment, environmental conservation, education, and cultural preservation, with a particular focus on reducing noise pollution in Indian cities.[1]

Programs and Initiatives[edit]

NSWF states that its core programs include:

  • Noise Pollution Awareness: school workshops, community sessions, and expert talks on the health and environmental effects of noise.[1]
  • Real-Time Noise Monitoring: collection of decibel-level data during festivals, traffic hours, and public events, intended to support research and policy advocacy.[1]
  • Behavioural Change Campaigns: initiatives encouraging reduced horn use and other responsible sound practices.[1]
  • Women's Wellness and Empowerment Sessions.[2]

Outreach Activities[edit]

Reported outreach activities include community events such as an Independence Day celebration for children at an orphanage in 2023, tree-plantation drives, and a "goodwill ambassador" program involving individuals who support the organization's noise-pollution and environmental awareness goals.[2][3]

Recognition[edit]

The organization states that it has been "internationally featured by BBC on People Fixing the World" and "highlighted in a France 24 documentary on noise pollution in India."[1] As of this draft, no independent broadcast record, transcript, or third-party reference confirming these claims has been located; they are presented here as organization-reported statements only and should not be treated as independently verified.
